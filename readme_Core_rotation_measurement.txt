### Project: Core_rotation_measurement.py

I completed this project during my PhD thesis (2015 - 2018).


### Project overview

This project aims at measuring a physical parameter from a power spectrum. More specifically, we want to measure the rate at which the core of stars rotates on itself, i.e. the core rotation of stars. This is made possible by the propagation of internal waves inside stars that generates oscillation modes, allowing us to probe the interior of stars: this field is called asteroseismology, i.e. stellar seismology that works on principle similar as for Earth's seismology. In the case of red giant stars, which are evolved stars representing the future evolution stage of our Sun, some oscillation modes probe the deepest regions of the stars, i.e. their core, making possible to measure their core rotation rate provided that we correctly identify the oscillation modes that are sensitive to the physical conditions in the core.


### Dataset

The dataset is composed of power spectra obtained beforehand by taking the Fourier transform of the light curves of stars, i.e. the variation of their flux over time; indeed, the oscillation modes generate extremely faint and periodic variations of the flux of stars, making possible to identify the oscillation modes by their frequency in the power spectrum. The dataset is composed of power spectra for about 2000 red giant stars, i.e. one power spectrum per star. More specifically, the power spectra used here have been preprocessed beforehand, they are power spectra in stretched period rather than in frequency: they have been stretched in order to force a regular spacing of the oscillation modes using a physically-motivated basis, making much easier the measurement of the core rotation rate. Here, only one power spectrum is provided as an example: ./Stars/stretched_spectrum_KIC_*.txt. The file ./Stars/parameters_KIC_*.txt contains physical parameters needed to exploit the power spectrum.


### Methodology

## Data preprocessing

Data preprocessing steps involve detecting the oscillation modes of interest to identify the signature of the core rotation rate: identifying the frequency range corresponding to the oscillation modes that are sensitive to the physical conditions in the core, selecting the peaks that are significantly enough above the background level, grouping them to identify the oscillation modes because one single oscillation mode can result in more than one peak due to the stochastic nature of oscillations and the non-zero frequency resolution, and selecting the most significant oscillation modes that will be used to fit the model. A later iterative step consists in manually setting the optimal level above the background above which we consider the oscillation modes for the fit. To that end, a plot called an échelle diagram is used as a visual tool to optimize the visualization of the components generated by rotation while minimizing the number of oscillation modes used to fit the model.


## Model

The model is composed of a collection of synthetic frequencies and stretched periods that account for the effect of rotation based on physical equations. 3240 different configurations are built, corresponding to 45 values of the core rotation rate, 24 values of the period spacing used to fold the power spectrum, and three rotational configurations where either 1, 2 or 3 rotational components are visible.


## Fitting the model to the data

The model is fitted to the data by centering the synthetic pattern on the observed one using the most significant modes; each of the 3240 configurations is tested. The number of oscillation modes close enough to the model is counted, and the reduced chi-square is computed. The échelle diagrams showing the data and the best-fitting models with 1, 2 and 3 rotational components are saved in ./Figures_rotation_intermediate/ and used as a visual tool to provide as an input the number of optimal rotational components visible: 1, 2 or 3. Once the number of rotational components is fixed, the best corresponding configuration is selected as the configuration maximizing the number of modes aligned with the synthetic pattern; if several configurations correspond to the same maximum number of modes aligned with the synthetic pattern, the configuration minimizing the reduced chi-square is selected as the best configuration.


### Results

The results are saved in the file Results.txt; KIC is the identifier of the star analyzed; Dnu is the large frequency separation between radial pressure oscillation modes; nu_max is the frequency of maximum oscillation power; Dpi1 is the period spacing associated to dipole gravity oscillation modes; dnurot_core is the core rotation rate in frequency; number_of_components is the number of visible rotational components in the échelle diagram. The final échelle diagram showing the data and the best-fitting model is also saved as Echelle_diagram_KIC_*.pdf.


### Key findings

This project led to the publication of a scientific article in the international review Astronomy and Astrophysics in 2018: Gehan et al. 2018, A&A, vol. 616, A24. The results presented in this paper are central to the astrophysics community that widely relies on them, as testified by the high number of citations (about 190) by other publications.
